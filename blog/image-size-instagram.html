Check the current publishing interface

Social platforms can change upload handling, crops, and recommendations. Treat any dimensions in a guide as a practical starting point and confirm them in Instagram’s current composer before publishing. The visible crop preview matters more than an old template. Keep important text and faces away from edges that may be clipped in a feed preview or when a post is shown in another context.

Begin with the original image and identify the placement: square or landscape feed image, portrait feed image, Story, Reel cover, or profile picture. These placements have different shapes. A square crop of a portrait photograph removes a substantial amount of vertical scene, so decide the crop intentionally rather than letting an upload screen make the decision unexpectedly.

Aspect ratio before pixel count

Aspect ratio describes width relative to height. A 4:5 portrait image is tall; a 1:1 image is square; a 1.91:1 image is landscape. If an image has an incompatible ratio, the platform may crop it, fit it with padding, or display a preview crop. Use a copy for social publishing so the full-resolution master remains available.

Pixel dimensions matter after composition. A 1080 × 1350 image has a 4:5 ratio and is a common working canvas for portrait feed designs. A square 1080 × 1080 canvas is easy to preview. Instagram’s Help Center currently says that photos 320–1080 pixels wide keep their original resolution when their ratio is between 1.91:1 and 3:4; outside that range, cropping may occur. See the official photo-resolution guidance before publishing. These are current handling notes, not a guarantee for every placement or account flow.

Practical export settings

Export photographs as JPEG or WebP only if the upload workflow accepts the format reliably. For compatibility across photo upload paths, JPEG is a familiar choice. For text-heavy artwork, inspect small lettering after upload because platform recompression can soften fine details. Avoid tiny text near the edge, and test the actual mobile view if the post is important.

Use the highest-quality source available, resize a copy to the intended canvas, and avoid repeated saves. If you need to convert a PNG graphic, try PNG to JPG for a solid background or PNG to WebP when transparency or the upload path supports it. Never choose JPG if you need transparent pixels.

Safe composition

Compose with a margin around important content. An image can be square in the grid but shown with another preview crop in some surfaces. Keep logos, captions, and faces away from extreme top and bottom edges. For a multi-image carousel, consistent ratios help the sequence feel intentional and prevent the frame from shifting between slides.

For a Story or Reel cover, design the central safe zone first. Interface overlays, captions, and buttons can occupy edges. Put decorative background there and preserve the focal content centrally. If the same artwork must work as a feed post and a vertical cover, create separate crops from the source rather than stretching one layout.

Quality after upload

The platform may resize and recompress uploads. Your local output therefore may not be identical to the final displayed rendition. Start from a sharp original, choose reasonable dimensions, and avoid excessive compression before uploading; two lossy encoding steps can soften details more than one. For text or line art, use a suitable graphics format during design and inspect the platform rendition at ordinary viewing scale.

If an image looks soft, distinguish source blur from platform processing. Check the original, local export, and published version. If the local export already looks poor, adjust the format or quality before upload. If only the published version differs, test another accepted format and inspect current platform behavior. A practical checklist

Before publishing, check ratio, orientation, crop, safe margins, and readable text. Keep the original master. Export a copy at a pixel size appropriate to the placement rather than uploading an oversized camera original. Check file size if your connection is slow or the upload form imposes a cap. Preview the actual post on a phone when layout accuracy matters.

Do not assume a file extension dictates quality or platform acceptance. Upload validation can depend on file encoding, not just its name. Avoid aggressive “under a tiny size” targets when the post is a photograph with fine texture. Use the 100 KB compressor only when a real limit calls for it, and review the result before posting.

Conclusion

A useful Instagram export is a well-composed copy in a shape that suits its placement, encoded clearly and checked in the current preview. Keep the master and revisit platform guidance when its publishing flow changes.

Frequently asked questions

Which format creates the smallest image?

It depends on image content, dimensions, encoder, and quality. Compare exports from the same original and inspect at the intended size.

Will conversion restore lost detail?

No. A conversion cannot recreate information discarded during an earlier lossy export. Return to the original when available.

Should I delete my original?

Keep it if you may need future edits, a different crop, or another format. Delivery copies are not source masters.
